The artifact under review (sha256 fece1121311d…, R011 integrated candidate A) is one self-contained file. Its core bet is a namespaced directory of (ns_id, local_id) resources, one append-only cursor-stream log per resource, an adapter-owned open CapMap, ns-scoped typed actions, a typed pump on the adapter handle, and exactly one delivery rule: subscribe(id, from_cursor=c) replays the log's retained envelopes with seq at or above c in increasing order, then continues live, with c chosen by the subscriber (0 for a just-spawned head, cursor_resolve for the live edge, saved_last_seen + 1 while recorded_ns_id matches, else 0). I checked the three things the contract singles out. Storage: the replay guarantee now names its store in the artifact's own words — R11.3 "Every resource has one cursor-stream log" — with a retention rule ("retained from the moment pump appends it until the resource is retired or its namespace torn down. There is no truncation, expiry or window while the resource exists"), two named disposal points, and a stated cost ("A resource's log grows with every envelope it has ever emitted, for the life of the resource"). The bound is a lifetime, not a size; that is declared rather than hidden, and only the producing adapter can tighten it. Rendering: R11.5's four-variant table plus the invariant "An outcome the host has not confirmed may never be rendered as a current value or a current state" does let a user tell confirmed-current from outcome-unknown, and an unregistered payload schema becomes an explicit opaque row rather than a blank pane. It does not let a user determine running, done or failed from the host alone: those are adapter-declared read/status fields, which the host echoes without meaning ("status, state, additions are labels it echoes, never tokens it interprets"), and with no such action registered the view truthfully says the value cannot be obtained (R11.9). That is the artifact's own admission and its weakest user-visible point; it is honest, and R11.6 item 6 does at least name the adapter's duty to register read/status, so the cost is stated. Where the artifact is thin: six of the twelve trajectories (S06, S07, S08, S09, S10, S12) are compressed arrow chains, not step tables. They carry ordered sequences but name an owner for only some steps and give deletion consequences per mechanism rather than per step, so under the contract's own three-part test they are insufficient_evidence, even though the R11.7 preamble claims "every other scenario carries its ordered step sequence, per-step owner, and the deletion consequence for each core step it uses". S07's selection half is carried only by the sentence "no resolver matches" plus R11.5's cross-reference to a section that is not in this artifact ("Extension selection … unchanged from §R8.4"). S01 is worse than thin: its step order makes the subscription post-date the events it is meant to render, and step 3 calls handle_for, a function that appears nowhere in R11.2. S03's step-4 delete line attributes the step to the same-ns rule, a permission, rather than to the R11.3 retention clause that actually enables the catch-up. I ruled step tables trajectory_ok and compressed chains insufficient_evidence, consistently; a reader can reject me by producing the missing per-step owner and delete line in those six rows.

BOUNDARY_SCAN: I found no service, model, harness or conversation assumption inside the core types — R11.2's Envelope is { resource_id, seq, ts, kind, payload_schema_id, payload } with no role, turn, sender, assistant or message_type field (S04's no-chat proof rests on exactly this), SubscriberScope and NamespaceHandle expose only fixed typed methods, and R11.1 states the core "never reads a payload, never branches on a CapMap string, and names no capability of its own"; the one place the core holds content is the disclosed log ("What it does hold is the log — and this round states that plainly instead of denying it"), which is a store without interpretation, not a service or model. I did find one relocated burden: R11.5 says "Extension selection, the absent→fallback / throws→error-boundary / uninstall→host-closes-scope guarantees, and the pair gesture are unchanged from §R8.4", and §R8.4 is not in this artifact, so S07's selection rule and S10's uninstall guarantee are carried by an out-of-artifact cross-reference; no extension-author cost section exists anywhere here (R11.6 is "Adapter boundary rules" only), and the extension author's duties (cursor persistence, live-edge versus 0 choice, unmount close, rendering the four variants) appear scattered but never costed. The adapter-side cost of registering read/status is named (R11.6 item 6) and admitted in R11.9, so that specific relocation is paid.
